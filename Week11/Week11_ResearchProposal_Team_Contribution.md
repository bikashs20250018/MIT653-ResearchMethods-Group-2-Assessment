# 1. Introduction and Background

**Contributor:** Bikash Shrestha (S20250018)

The rapid development and use of AI in many fields has drastically
changed the educational environment in recent years. These days, online
resources provide students with much more than simply readings,
lectures, and tests. AI-driven personalisation is therefore a
significant development since it seeks to provide students with a
personalised learning experience rather than a standardised one that
ensures the same learning experience for everyone. New educational
technologies can analyse your interactions and, based on those, offer
recommendations with resources, adjust the level of difficulty of the
content, give feedback automatically, and offer individualised learning
trajectories.

The majority of traditional classroom and online learning options use a
fairly standardised method of instruction. The same learning resources,
exercises, and tests can be given to students in a preset order.
Although standardisation provides uniformity, learners differ in their
past knowledge, learning speed, motivations, interests, and ability to
understand certain topics. By modifying the learning environment based
on student data, adaptive learning systems attempt to address these
disparities.

Personalisation might include suggesting educational resources, changing
the difficulty of the questions, and providing additional explanations
and feedback based on the performance of the learners \[1\]. AI has the
ability to speed up these procedures as algorithms are capable of
automatically processing vast volumes of learner data and making
recommendations. One of the uses of AI in education that has been noted
in recent research is personalised learning \[2\].

However, not every AI element has an influence on learning.
Personalisation of content does not improve student learning. Similarly,
students can like suggestions based on their own traits but be concerned
about the volume of information gathered or the proposals' impartiality.
Therefore, any educational advantages must be weighed against privacy,
prejudice, uneven access, and over-reliance on technology \[3\].

The purpose of this study is to investigate these elements via the
perspectives and experiences of students using AI-supported online
learning environments. Adaptive learning algorithms, tailored material
delivery, adaptive evaluation, and AI-generated feedback will be the
study's main areas of interest.

## 1.1 Research Issue

The literature currently in publication suggests that adaptive and
personalised learning can improve learning and engagement, but the
effectiveness of AI-based personalisation will depend on the learners'
experiences and the way the technology is used. It is still necessary to
investigate the relationship between AI personalisation and other facets
of educational efficacy outside of academics.

Investigating how personalised learning affects motivation, engagement,
and satisfaction as well as perceived learning efficacy is particularly
important. Ethical and practical concerns may also have an impact on
students' desire to use the AI-powered platform.

Therefore, the advantages and issues of AI-supported personalised
e-learning will be examined in this study.

## 1.2 Research Question

### Main Research Question

How well do AI-powered e-learning systems enable personalised learning
through tailored content distribution and adaptive learning algorithms?

### Questions for Sub-Research

-   What effects does AI personalisation have on students' motivation,
    engagement, and satisfaction?
-   What is the relationship between student learning performance and
    personalised adaptive learning?
-   How do students feel about AI-generated feedback, adaptive
    assessment, and personalised content?
-   What worries do students have regarding AI-powered personalised
    learning in terms of privacy, fairness, accessibility, and
    dependence?
-   What elements influence the efficacy of personalised e-learning
    systems driven by AI?
-   What is the impact of personalised content distribution on students'
    inclination to engage in online learning activities?
-   What effects does adaptive learning have on students' capacity to
    study at their own speed?
-   How helpful do students think AI-generated suggestions are for
    choosing relevant educational materials?
-   What is the impact of AI-personalized learning content relevancy on
    students' e-learning platform satisfaction?
-   What impact does adjustable difficulty have on students'
    self-assurance when they complete online learning tasks?
-   How accurate and helpful do students think AI-generated feedback is?
-   What impact does the promptness of AI-generated feedback have on
    students' perceptions of the efficacy of their education?
-   In contrast to standardised online tests, how do students see
    adaptive assessments?
-   What aspects of AI-powered personalised learning systems affect
    students' trust?
-   How do students' experiences with AI-powered personalised learning
    depend on their level of digital literacy?
-   What effect does perceived usability have on students' propensity to
    stick with AI-powered e-learning platforms?
-   What worries do students have regarding the security and privacy of
    the personal learning information that AI-powered systems gather?
-   How do students see algorithmic bias and fairness in suggestions for
    personalised learning produced by AI?
-   What effects do technological availability, accessibility, and
    reliance on AI have on students' experiences with customised
    e-learning?
-   How well do AI-powered e-learning systems enable personalised
    learning through tailored content distribution and adaptive learning
    algorithms?

## 1.3 Research Goals

The purpose of this study is to look at the relationship between
    academic or perceived learning performance and the usage of
    AI-supported personalised learning. The following goals will continue to be pursued by the research:

-   To investigate the effects of AI-powered personalisation in online
    learning environments.
-   To look at the relationship between engagement and adaptive content
    delivery.
-   To investigate the relationship between student motivation and
    satisfaction and the use of AI in personalised learning.
-   To determine what worries students, have about algorithmic
    unfairness, privacy, accessibility, and reliance on AI.
-   To provide suggestions for the appropriate and successful
    application of AI-based personalised learning.

## 1.4 Research Theories

The following theories will be examined in the quantitative section:

-   **H1:** There is a strong and favourable relationship between
    student engagement and AI-driven personalised material delivery.
-   **H2:** There is a favourable correlation between student motivation
    and the usage of AI to provide personalised material.
-   **H3:** Student happiness is positively correlated with the adoption
    of AI-driven personalised learning.
-   **H4:** Students are more likely to get successful learning outcomes
    if they believe that adaptive learning is effective.
-   **H5:** The results showed a substantial positive correlation
    between perceived learning efficacy and the quality of the
    AI-generated personalised feedback.

These hypotheses are statements of predicted relationships that will be
examined using appropriate statistical techniques. A positive
association does not imply causality, according to the research.
